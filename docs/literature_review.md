# Literature and source-code review

Inspected 5 November 2026. Results in other repositories are **their claims**, not reproduced GRAIL results. No third-party source code was copied into this package.

The first accident repository reports binary XGBoost holdout ROC-AUC 0.702 and severe F1 0.22. These are not comparable to our four-class synthetic metrics. Its README's “6 GB to 1.3 GB ≈29% reduction” is arithmetically inconsistent. Feature importance alone does not prove genuine rather than shortcut signal.

SAE-XCrash calibrates on validation but chooses isotonic vs Platt using that same calibration set's ECE, favoring flexible fits. Its US disruption outcome and UK injury-severity target are different constructs; failure of transfer cannot be attributed solely to spatial memorization. The README says submitted, while a [publisher page](https://www.mdpi.com/2076-3417/16/12/5876) was found; repository publication metadata is stale. The dataset license should be checked against the source, not copied from that README.

## Method synthesis

| Method and primary source | Mathematical idea / implementation | Strength, limitation and relevance | Proposed improvement / originality |
|---|---|---|---|
| [CORAL, Cao et al. 2020](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.patrec.2020.11.008) | Shared latent score and K−1 threshold BCE tasks | Ordered output; restrictive shared slope. Appropriate only with comparable disruption labels. | Explicitly parameterize ordered gaps. This is a standard constraint, not novelty. |
| [CORN, Shi et al. 2023](https://arxiv.org/abs/2111.08851) | Conditional risk-set BCE; chain products | More flexible output relations; higher-threshold risk sets are small. | Baseline checks whether shared-score restrictions hurt rare grades. No novel claim. |
| [Group DRO, Sagawa et al. 2020](https://arxiv.org/abs/1911.08731) | min max weighted group risk; exponentiated updates | Can protect known difficult groups; noisy/small groups and unseen domains remain problematic. | Shrink group risks, KL-regularize weights, evaluate explicit ablations. Components already established. |
| [TabM, Gorishniy et al. ICLR 2025](https://proceedings.iclr.cc/paper_files/paper/2025/hash/c1ba41c694834aeef91ae161711d4939-Abstract-Conference.html) | Parameter-efficient MLP ensemble | Strong tabular baseline; added compute/tuning. | Must eventually compare before attributing gains to a custom encoder. No custom interaction encoder implemented without evidence. |
| [Logit adjustment, Menon et al. 2021](https://research.google/pubs/long-tail-learning-via-logit-adjustment/) | Add log class-prior offsets to logits | Targets long-tail decisions; probabilities and desired decision costs must be distinguished. | Current study uses class weights and controlled sampling; logit adjustment is a future ablation, not completed. |
| [DomainBed, Gulrajani & Lopez-Paz 2021](https://arxiv.org/abs/2007.01434) | Controlled domain-generalization evaluation and model selection | Carefully tuned ERM is strong; held-out domain tuning can distort claims. | Separate validation states and final test states. Geography is only a proxy for mechanisms. |
| [Temperature scaling, Guo et al. 2017](https://proceedings.mlr.press/v70/guo17a.html) | One positive temperature minimizes held-out NLL | Low variance; cannot fix every class/group bias or distribution shift. | Independent calibration role; test pre/post Brier, NLL and ECE. Established technique. |
| [Selective classification, Geifman & El-Yaniv 2017](https://proceedings.neurips.cc/paper/2017/hash/4a8423d5e91fda00bb7e46540e2b0cf1-Abstract.html) | Abstain on uncertain predictions, evaluate risk vs coverage | Makes rejection cost visible; entropy need not rank errors under shift. | Record group-wise failure/review rankings; no coverage guarantee claimed. |
| [Learning to reweight, Ren et al. 2018](https://proceedings.mlr.press/v80/ren18a.html) | Bilevel weights chosen by trusted validation-gradient alignment | Can address noise/imbalance; needs trustworthy representative validation and extra gradients. | Deferred until reliable labels and meta-validation states exist. Adaptive loss learning already exists. |
| [GCE, Zhang & Sabuncu 2018](https://papers.neurips.cc/paper_files/paper/2018/hash/f2925f97bc13ad2852a7a551802feea0-Abstract.html) | (1−p_y^q)/q interpolates CE and MAE-like robust loss | Reduces outlier dominance; can underlearn hard rare examples. | Deferred: cannot treat difficult minority incidents as mislabeled by default. |
| [Robust deep ordinal regression, Garg & Manwani 2020](https://proceedings.mlr.press/v129/garg20a/garg20a.pdf) | Noise-aware ordinal learning | Direct precedent for robust ordinal objectives; depends on noise assumptions. | Label-transition modelling needs independent evidence, not guessed relabeling. |
| [SCARF, Bahri et al. 2022](https://arxiv.org/abs/2106.15147) | Contrast original rows and feature-corrupted views before supervised fitting | Uses unlabeled tabular structure; corruption may violate weather/road dependencies. | Future training-only pretraining experiment. Not implemented. |
| [Adaptive conformal inference, Gibbs & Candès 2021](https://arxiv.org/abs/2106.00170) | Update miscoverage control with observed feedback | Useful under changing streams; requires feedback and has specific long-run guarantees. | Future ordinal prediction sets; static entropy is not a substitute for conformal guarantees. |

## Independent challenge and ranked additions

1. **Provider × year × state label audit, highest priority.** A change in marginal P(Y=3) can reflect changing P(provider,state), not a changed definition. Compare within strata and standardize to an overlapping reference mixture. Falsifier: the apparent break disappears after mixture adjustment. Low implementation cost; full data blocked.
2. **Simple tree/ERM baselines and geographic feature removal.** Hypothesis: coordinates/state/provider shortcuts explain transfer gaps. Fit identical splits with/without each family; compare degradation, not only in-domain scores. Implemented primary/no-geography/provider/year modes. No causal identification.
3. **Shrinkage-regularized geographic objective.** Hypothesis: noisy finite-sample group means destabilize DRO. Compare τ=0 and λ=α=0 ablations, all with the same ordinal head. Implemented. A negative result is valuable; small-group harm must be inspected.
4. **Provider-stratified and synthetic label-shift stress tests.** Hypothesis: ordinal gains disappear under inconsistent label maps. Medium cost; add controlled perturbations only to training labels and retain untouched evaluation labels. Recommended next; no hidden relabeling performed.
5. **TabM / small ensembles before a bespoke encoder.** Hypothesis: regularized ensembling improves both NLL and rare-class recall. Medium compute; inspected, not yet benchmarked.
6. **Ordinal conformal sets / abstention with feedback.** Hypothesis: interval-like sets expose ambiguity better than entropy alone. Medium cost; coverage can fail under shift. Deferred until a credible calibration population exists.
7. **Meta-reweighting or SCARF pretraining.** High cost and extra assumptions. Require independent trustworthy meta-validation or a train-only unlabeled pool. Do not implement merely to make the name more distinctive.

### Design answers

Ordinal modelling is semantically plausible but conditional on label consistency. States are administratively useful, heterogeneous groups; they are not reliable causal domains. Sample count measures estimation precision, not truthfulness. Adaptive weights are not assumed superior. Provider coverage can explain geographic effects. A simpler logistic/tree model may match or beat GRAIL. Reliability of the target is more promising to investigate first than a new feature-interaction layer. Novelty remains unestablished after this scoped review; it is not an exhaustive prior-art search.
